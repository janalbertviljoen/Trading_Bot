# Buffett reference: the investor's method

How Midas chooses and holds companies, following Warren Buffett's method as Buffett
describes it in his letters to Berkshire Hathaway shareholders. Each principle is stated
in our own words and cited to the letter's year, with a brief quotation where the
wording matters. Midas quotes Buffett; it never speaks as him or implies his
endorsement.

The letters are free to read on Berkshire Hathaway's website,
[berkshirehathaway.com/letters](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html),
1977 to 2024. Benjamin Graham, *The Intelligent Investor*, remains the reference for
general questions; Buffett called buying it "the best" of all his investments (2013).

## How to think about a share

1. **A share is part of a business.** Market prices are offers from "a remarkably
   accommodating fellow named Mr. Market", Graham's image, who names a price every day;
   the investor may take it or ignore it (1987). Falling prices are a chance to buy, not
   a verdict (2008).
2. **Price is not value.** "Price is what you pay; value is what you get", Graham's
   lesson as Buffett repeats it (2008, 2013). Value is intrinsic value: the cash a
   business will produce, discounted; the term runs through almost every letter.
3. **Owner earnings, not reported earnings.** What an owner can take out: reported
   earnings plus depreciation and similar non-cash charges, less the capital spending
   the business needs to keep its position (1986).

## What to buy

4. **Quality before cheapness.** "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair
   price than a fair company at a wonderful price" (1989).
5. **A durable moat.** "A truly great business must have an enduring 'moat' that
   protects excellent returns on invested capital" (2007); a durable competitive
   advantage (1999, 2007, 2014).
6. **Stay within your circle of competence.** Judge only businesses you can understand;
   "knowing its boundaries, however, is vital" (1996).
7. **A margin of safety in the price.** If the value is only slightly above the price,
   do not buy; the principle Graham emphasised (1992).

## How to hold

8. **Hold for the long term.** With outstanding businesses and managements, "our
   favorite holding period is forever" (1988). The opposite habit, selling winners and
   keeping losers, is the mistake to avoid.
9. **Be contrarian about mood, not about facts.** The aim is "to be fearful when others are
   greedy and to be greedy only when others are fearful" (1986).
10. **Know your risks before the crisis.** "You only find out who is swimming naked when
    the tide goes out" (2001).

## For most investors: the index fund

11. **Low-cost index funds beat most professionals.** "The best way to own common stocks
    is through an index fund that charges minimal fees" (1996); fees charged by
    "helpers" leave investors worse off in aggregate (2016, where Buffett recounts his
    ten-year bet against hedge funds).
12. **Buffett's own instruction.** For the trust that will hold his wife's inheritance:
    "Put 10% of the cash in short-term government bonds and 90% in a very low-cost S&P 500
    index fund" (2013).

## How Midas uses this reference

- **Choosing companies:** the Newtonian models screen on Graham's rules; Midas explains a
  pick in Buffett's terms where they apply: owner earnings, moat, margin of safety, the
  price against the value.
- **The core and the satellite:** Buffett's 90/10 instruction (2013) is the same split
  as the 40-year end of the starting investor's path, and his advice that most investors
  should own a low-cost index fund (1996) is why the core comes first and the satellite
  stays small.
- **Behaviour:** buy and hold, ignore Mr. Market's moods, and know the risks before the
  tide goes out.
- **Limits:** quotations stay brief and cited by year; a member who wants more reads the
  letter itself.
